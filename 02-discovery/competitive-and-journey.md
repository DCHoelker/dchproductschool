# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A consumer with limited time and an uncertain income who is trying to deal with a debt online.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Get a clear answer on whether a manageable payment plan is possible, and start one they can keep.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** They are asked to commit to a monthly amount they cannot predict, and they fear that one wrong answer or one missed payment will make everything due at once. So they under-report, abandon, or call for reassurance instead of finishing online.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Phone the contact centre, not for information but for reassurance that they are not making a mistake

They may check their own bank balance or payslips elsewhere before committing, or delay until payday. P9 hints at the timing piece, but no quote describes a tool or a manual calculation.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Observed steps (from P1, P3, P5, P6, P7, P8, P10)

Enter the portal from a text, usually during a short break (P1).
Hit the affordability question and cannot pick between best month and worst month (P3).
Choose a defensive answer. They understate income "just to be safe" (P8), or accept the default slider value because it feels like what everyone picks (P4).
Hit a second blocker such as a payslip request they cannot meet because part of their pay is in cash (P6), or an unexplained income question (P5).
Exit without finishing, either closing the portal or never starting because of the fear of acceleration after one missed payment (P7).
Phone the contact centre, not for information but for reassurance that they are not making a mistake (P10).
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Hit a second blocker such as a payslip request they cannot meet because part of their pay is in cash (P6), or an unexplained income question (P5).
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** "They ask me what I can afford per month. My pay changes every month. Do I put my best month? My worst month? Whatever I put, I feel like I'm signing up to it forever."
